# `MBRF004` triage — all 120, live city, 2026-08-19

**Status:** classification. **No bead was mutated to produce this document, and
no source link was backfilled.** Every measurement below is a read
(`mctl briefs list|doctor`, `mctl_core.beads.read_beads`) across all **17
registered rigs including `hq`**.

**Scope:** the **120 of 141 open** decision beads on which `MBRF004` ("brief
bead has no source dependency", POLICY B2.1) fires. `MBRF004` is `Severity.ERROR`
and `briefs.py:_blocking_diagnostic` refuses on any ERROR, so those 120 beads
cannot be `adjudicate`d, `defer`red, or `dispatch-work`ed. It is the largest
single block on *acting* on briefs, as distinct from *reading* them.

---

## 0. The finding: half of them are not briefs, and B2.1 already said so

POLICY B2.1's Definitions paragraph (`subdomains/brief-system/POLICY.md:53-56`)
carves out a class in as many words:

> **Decision bead.** A bead of type `decision` … **The brief bead IS the
> decision bead** — one bead per brief. Decision beads created for OTHER
> purposes (push authorizations, kill-switch engagement/release, non-brief
> adjudications) remain their own standalone beads; only the brief/decision-bead
> pairing is collapsed.

`Bead.is_brief` is `issue_type == "decision"` with no discriminator
(`beads.py:56-58`), so that sentence had never been implemented. A push receipt
was therefore counted as a brief and then flagged for lacking a source
dependency — **which it cannot have, because it is not deciding about another
bead.** `MBRF004` on a push receipt is the checker asking a question that does
not apply to the thing it is asking about.

**49 of the 120 leave the population under B2.1's own rule.** The remaining
**71 stay**, and this document reports them rather than repairing them.

## 2. What was exempted, and by which rule

### 2.1 Push authorizations — 49 of the 120 (`MBRF054`)

B2.1 class 1. These are receipts written by the `authorize-git-operation` skill
(`skills/authorize-git-operation/SKILL.md:157-163`). Three **structured**
markers identify them, all of them the skill's own output:

| marker | shape | n (of the 120) |
|---|---|---:|
| receipt sentence | `Authorization gate invoked via authorize-git-operation skill.` at the head of `description`/`notes`/`design` | 40 |
| notes template | `Operation: <type>. … Verdict: <AUTHORIZED\|DENIED>` (`--notes` in Step 3) | 6 |
| title tag | `[git-auth]` / `[authorize-git]` | 3 |

The notes template requires **both** keys, in the skill's order, and the verdict
word must be the skill's `AUTHORIZED`/`DENIED` enum rather than B2.2's
`approve`/`reject`/`revise`/`defer`. Neither key is distinctive alone: real
briefs describe operations, and real briefs record verdicts.

### 2.2 Kill-switch engagement/release — 0 of the 120 (`MBRF055`)

B2.1 class 2, and N5: "Engaging or releasing a kill switch requires explicit
authorization, **recorded as a STANDALONE decision bead** … its own bead, not a
brief verdict; unaffected by the one-bead model" (`POLICY.md:409-415`).

There is exactly **one** such bead in the live 280 — `gt-0i99e`, *DECISION:
release no-brainer kill-switch (auto_merge_enabled false->true)* — and it is
**closed**, so it is not among the 120. The discriminator is implemented anyway
because B2.1 names the class, but it moves this slice's number by zero and is
deliberately narrow: it requires the act to *govern* the switch (verb, then at
most two words, then the switch). `gsp-pxcu` is a policy-amendment bead whose
body discusses the switch hierarchy, and *"Should the rig-level kill-switch
default to engaged?"* is a brief asking about it. Neither is a record.

### 2.3 Non-brief adjudications — 0 exempted, deliberately

B2.1 class 3. **Not implemented, and that is the finding, not an omission.**

The phrase has no mechanical discriminator that is not circular: a
"non-brief adjudication" is a recorded decision that is not about a brief, and
nothing on these beads distinguishes that from a brief whose source link was
simply never added. Every candidate rule — *no `brief_*` metadata*, *title
starts with `POLICY:`*, *body has no `## §1 What is being decided`* — also
catches beads in §3.6 below that are plainly real briefs awaiting a verdict.

Widening the discriminator until the number looked better is the exact failure
this triage exists to avoid, so the 71 stay in the population and `MBRF004`
keeps firing on them.

## 4. A defect found and corrected on the way

Slice 2's `is_git_authorization_receipt` matched the bare string
`authorize-git-operation` **anywhere** in a bead's body. Measured across all 280
live decision beads, that exempted **5 real briefs** that merely mention the
skill in prose:

| bead | the prose that swept it out |
|---|---|
| `gt-s4r3a1` | "the human approval gate wherever one applies (e.g. `authorize-git-operation` for pushes/PRs)" |
| `gt-5fxchl` | "these need authorize-git-operation. Non-push decisions: …" |
| `gt-8lba37` | "this is the verdict - authorize-git-operation Step 1b: make optional" |
| `gt-gr755h` | "…config edits are BART-lane per LP1 + authorize-git-operation" |
| `gsp-qtadi` | "…onto gascity-packs main via BART, behind authorize-git-operation gate" |

The marker is now anchored to the head of the field the skill writes it into.
The cost is ~12 genuine receipts (§3.1) that stay in the population because
nothing structured identifies them. **That is the direction to fail in:** an
unexempted receipt is visible in the `MBRF004` list and a human resolves it,
whereas a real brief wrongly exempted disappears from every surface and nobody
finds out. Same reasoning as the no-guessing rule on source links.

**No regression to Slice 2.** Closed decision beads, before vs after, city-wide:
139 closed, `adjudicated + exempted` = **97 on both**. The change moves 2 closed
beads out of the exempt set and into the population, and both read as
`adjudicated` (61 → 63 adjudicated, 36 → 34 exempt, `malformed` unchanged at 42).
